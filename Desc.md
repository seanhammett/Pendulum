# À propos

## TL;DR

__Three physical clocks, recognizable in the rough form of a grandfather clock, but each has a face with abstracted physical movement. They face each other in the freefloating zone. Together, they play with the history of timekeeping, the emergence and destruction of patterns, and the chaotic movement of complex systems__

## Longitude Prize

In the first half of the age of sail, no instrument was capable of measuring a ship's progress around the earth. Determining latitude was simple: a measurement of the elevation of the sun at its highest point in the sky corresponds directly to a ship's distance from the equator. Longitude, the distance traveled parallel to the equator, however, was an unsolved problem before invention of the marine chronometer.

Precisely keeping a reference time while at sea, navigators could compare the time of the local solar noon to the known time of solar noon at the chronometer's reference frame. The difference in time corresponds to the longitude traversed.

This relationship between technology and nature is my main focus as an artist. We create instruments to measure a natural phenomenon, and the practical benefits it unlocks

# Overview

A set of three clock faces. 

![Sketch of clock arrangement](placeholder.png)

##### 1

Clock 1 is a single pendulum, with an escapement and a second hand. As gravity changes during the flight, the rate of the 'seconds' ticking by changes. In no gravity, time stops entirely. The pendulum turns an arc, with only the inertia it had when gravity stopped

##### 2

Clock 2 is a double pendulum. When gravity is present, it's movement is chaotic. The increase in gravity during the flight changes the pendulums' periods and adds energy to the system. 

The release of gravity completely and abruptly removes the chaotic nature of the system. The inertia of the pendulums takes over, and in place of the wild, whip-like movements of the gravity-bound system, we see graceful repeating patterns.

##### 3

Clock 3 is a triple pendulum. Regardless of gravity, it is chaotic, wild, and dynamic. Patterns briefly appear during weightlessness, but break again under their own inertia.

# Technical details

## Simulator

I have created pendulum simulator, available here: <https://www.seanhammett.com>. It shows the behaviour of the clocks during the cyclical gravity of the flight. 

The clocks must be constructed with an absolutely minimal amount of friction, both the mechanical friction in the bearings, and the air friction of the pendulum arms. (This friction is approximated with a single value in the simulator).

## Observations

The pendulums are strongly influenced by the timing of the gravity changes. Tracking the kinetic and potential energy of each as their moving shows the tradoff between the two; as gravity drops to zero, the potential energy does as well. Depending on the height of the masses when the gravity returns

## Construction

The clocks will be constructed with materials that echo the revolutionary clocks of John Harrison, without entering into pastiche. Engraved metal faces with exposed, celebrated linkages. These clocks will be visually significantly simpler than those of Harrison.

Primary colors (a distinct departure from Harrison) differentiate between the new clocks, emphasize the movement of the nodes, and add an element of playful ness to the work.

## Post production

The simulator remains publicly available. A series of prints representing different cycles of weightlessness are created from the flight data, or from the simulations.

The clocks themselves retain their sculptural quality, and can be displayed as desired

>An 'anti-gravtiy' version of each clock could be made: adding a balanced counterweight to each pendulum would negate the gravitational effect, and recreate the weightless behavior
